CounterSignal 0.2.0 strengthens the benchmark and fixes the issues found when 0.1.0 was reviewed independently of its development. The 0.1.0 benchmark figures are superseded.

## Benchmark

* Second dataset: UCI Sepsis Survival Minimal Clinical Records (110,204 admissions, in hospital death), alongside UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals.
* Gradient boosting models alongside logistic regression, for four configurations.
* Comparison with an independent implementation of the Feng et al. score based CUSUM, calibrated on untreated patients from a run-in under the deployment policy, as the authors' companion code calibrates on standard of care patients.
* Every method is scored against the population truth of each month, which is now exact because months are drawn with replacement from a known population. 0.1.0 scored against each month's own sample, which overstated interval coverage.
* Drift is sized so the true O/E reaches 1.35, outside the acceptable range.
* New scenario: treatment depends on a factor the monitor is not given.
* Tables in the README and docs/benchmark.md are generated from the results and checked in CI.

Headline: CounterSignal caught drift among treated patients in at least 99.5% of deployments in every configuration, most often in the second month of the change, against 16.0% to 88.0% for the other monitors. With the model unchanged it alarmed in 4.5% to 9.0% of deployments, similar to the untreated only and Feng et al. monitors but above the planned 5% in three of the four configurations (up to 9.0%, about 2.6 standard errors above); standard monitoring alarmed in 95.0% to 100.0%. When treatment also depended on a factor the monitor was not given, its alarm rate rose to 12.0% on the sepsis data.

## Fixes

* Months are processed in sorted label order, not file order.
* Inputs are validated; months with no untreated patients get a "no untreated patients" verdict; a month is evaluated only once all its outcomes are recorded.
* The treatment model is refitted in every bootstrap resample.
* The window size needed to settle an undecided month is reported in patients, with accurate wording.
* The calibration intercept is the slope fixed intercept; Brier scores get intervals; the default alarm horizon is 12 months, with a warning beyond it.
* The population stability index now detects shifts in binary and discrete variables.
* The method notes state the alarm's exact null hypothesis, that calibration is absolute (O/E = 1), and the limits of the doubly robust estimate.
